About ELIZA - Historical Note

ELIZA: A Classic Milestone in Artificial Intelligence

ELIZA is one of the earliest and most influential chatbot programs in the history of artificial intelligence. It was created in the 1960s by Joseph Weizenbaum at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. The program was designed to demonstrate how a computer could simulate conversation with a human user using simple rules.

The most famous ELIZA script was called DOCTOR. It simulated a Rogerian psychotherapist by asking reflective questions. For example, when a user typed “I feel sad,” ELIZA could respond, “Why do you feel sad?” This created the impression that the computer understood the user, even though it was only following predefined rules.

ELIZA worked using pattern matching and keyword detection. It searched the user's input for important words or phrases and selected a suitable predefined response. If no specific pattern was found, it used general replies such as “Please tell me more” or “Can you explain that further?”

Key AI Concepts

  • Rule-based artificial intelligence
  • Pattern matching
  • Keyword detection
  • Predefined responses
  • Human-computer interaction
  • Early natural language processing

Historical Importance

  • Developed in the 1960s
  • Created by Joseph Weizenbaum
  • One of the first chatbot programs
  • Inspired later conversational systems
  • Introduced the idea of the ELIZA effect
  • Still used in AI education today

Why ELIZA Matters

ELIZA showed that even a simple rule-based system could create the illusion of intelligence. This helped researchers understand both the possibilities and the limitations of early conversational AI systems.
